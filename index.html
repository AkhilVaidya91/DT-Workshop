    Stage 5 · Interaction diagram
    
        
    Draw how people use your product
    
        
    Draw your interaction diagram on paper, take a photo, and give it to the LLM with the prompt below. The LLM turns your drawing into clear, end-to-end interaction flows.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Learn What is an interaction diagram?
    
        
    
          
    An interaction diagram (often drawn as a use-case diagram) shows who uses your product and what they can do with it. It is a map of interactions, not a step-by-step flowchart. It has four parts:
    
          
      
            
    • Actors are stick figures. Each one is a type of user from Stage 1, or an outside system such as a campus login, a calendar or a payment service. Put actors who start actions on the left. Put supporting actors and outside systems on the right.
      • 
            
    • The system boundary is a large box labelled with your product name. Everything inside the box is what your product does. Everything outside is not your product.
      • 
            
    • Use cases are ovals inside the box. Each oval is one goal an actor can reach, written as verb + noun: “Book a room”, “Send reminder”.
      • 
            
    • Lines connect each actor to every oval they take part in.
      • 
          
    
          
    How to draw yours: (1) Draw one stick figure per user type. (2) For each one, write 3–6 goals as ovals. (3) Draw the system box around the ovals. (4) Connect actors to ovals. (5) Add outside systems your product depends on.
    
          
    Check: every actor has at least one line, every oval connects to an actor, and every oval name starts with a verb.
    
        
    
        
      
    

      
    
        Think first
        
      
          
    • Did you include every user type from Stage 1? If one has no ovals, should they be in the product at all?
      • 
          
    • Which goals are shared by several users? Which belong to only one?
      • 
          
    • What outside services must your product talk to?
      •
